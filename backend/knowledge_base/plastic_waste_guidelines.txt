--- PLASTIC TYPES AND RECYCLABILITY ---

Thermoplastics (soften on heating, CAN be remolded/recycled):
- PET (1) - Polyethylene terephthalate: soft drink bottles, food containers
- HDPE (2) - High-density polyethylene: bottles, carry bags, milk pouches
- PVC (3) - Polyvinyl chloride: pipes, window profiles, non-food bottles
- LDPE (4) - Low-density polyethylene: plastic bags, containers
- PP (5) - Polypropylene: auto parts, food containers, dishware
- PS (6) - Polystyrene: cafeteria trays, plastic utensils, insulation

Thermosets (do NOT soften/remold on heating, generally NOT recyclable):
- SMC (Sheet Molding Compound), FRP (Fiber Reinforced Plastic), Bakelite

Note: plastic can typically only be recycled 3-4 times before quality
degrades too much for further recycling.

--- DO'S AND DON'TS FOR PLASTIC WASTE (household level) ---

DO:
- Use plastic carry bags/sheets that are 50 microns or thicker (thinner
  bags are banned under Plastic Waste Management Rules).
- Use virgin (not recycled/colored) plastic for storing or packaging food.
- Collect plastic waste in separate bins.
- Carry a cloth, jute, or compostable bag when shopping instead of taking a
  plastic carry bag.
- Buy food in glass jars or other non-plastic material where possible.

DON'T:
- Don't use carry bags/sheets thinner than 50 microns.
- Don't use colored or recycled plastic for storing/packaging food.
- Don't mix plastic waste with biodegradable (wet) waste.
- Don't burn plastic waste in open areas - this releases hazardous air
  pollutants and is linked to serious health issues.
- Don't litter plastic waste in drains, rivers, or sea/beaches - it can be
  ingested by animals, cattle, and marine life with fatal results.

--- DISPOSAL/RECOVERY ROUTES FOR SEGREGATED PLASTIC WASTE ---

Once properly segregated and handed to authorized collectors, plastic
waste in India is typically routed through one of these technologies
(household users don't need to do this themselves - this is for context on
where segregated plastic actually goes):
- Road construction: shredded plastic waste mixed into bitumen for road
  laying (reduces bitumen use, improves road durability).
- Co-processing in cement kilns: plastic used as alternate fuel at high
  temperature (1400-1500°C) with minimal harmful emissions.
- Conversion to fuel-oil (Refuse Derived Fuel): segregated plastic
  converted into fuel oil / diesel oil via depolymerisation.
- Plasma pyrolysis: high-temperature, oxygen-starved disintegration; can
  safely process chlorinated and multi-layer plastics that other methods
  can't handle.

--- ENVIRONMENTAL RISKS OF IMPROPER PLASTIC DISPOSAL ---

- Plastics are non-biodegradable and persist in the environment for
  thousands of years.
- Open burning releases hazardous air pollutants depending on the polymer
  type.
- Littered plastic chokes drains, makes land infertile, and can kill
  cattle/wildlife that ingest it.
- Improper dumping can leach metals and phthalates into soil and
  groundwater.
