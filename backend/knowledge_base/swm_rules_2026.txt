Published in the Gazette of India, 28 January 2026. In force from 1 April 2026.
Supersedes the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016.

--- SCOPE ---
These rules apply to every urban and rural local body and to every domestic,
institutional, commercial and non-residential solid waste generator in India.

These rules do NOT cover: industrial waste, hazardous waste, hazardous
chemicals, bio-medical waste, e-waste, battery waste, and radio-active waste.
These are governed by separate rules under the Environment (Protection) Act,
1986. (This is why e-waste and battery disposal guidance needs a different
source than this document.)

--- KEY WASTE CATEGORY DEFINITIONS ---

Wet waste: organic waste including kitchen waste, food waste, vegetable
waste, meat waste, fruit waste, flower waste, and similar biodegradable
waste.

Dry waste: waste other than wet waste, sanitary waste and special care
waste; includes recyclable waste and non-recyclable waste.

Sanitary waste: used diapers, sanitary towels/napkins, tampons, condoms,
incontinence sheets, and similar waste. Must be wrapped securely before
disposal, separate from dry/wet/special care waste.

Special care waste: discarded paint drums, pesticide cans/containers/
bottles, compact fluorescent lamps (CFL) or bulbs, tube lights, expired
medicines, broken mercury thermometers, waste batteries, used/waste needles
and syringes, contaminated gauze, or any other waste notified by CPCB,
generated at the household level.

Recyclable waste (sorting definition): separating components such as
paper (including paperboard), plastic, metal, glass, etc. from waste to
facilitate recycling, processing, or disposal.

Biodegradable waste: any organic material that can be degraded by
micro-organisms into simpler stable compounds.

Non-biodegradable waste: any waste that cannot be degraded by
micro-organisms into simpler stable compounds.

Horticultural waste: plant-based waste from parks/gardens (grass, wood
clippings, weeds, pruning, branches, dead leaves, tree trimmings) that
can't go in daily wet waste collection.

Construction and demolition waste: stored separately on the generator's own
premises and disposed of as per the Environment (Construction and
Demolition) Waste Management Rules, 2025.

--- DUTIES OF EVERY WASTE GENERATOR (Rule 5) ---

Every waste generator (i.e. every household/individual) shall:
- Segregate and store waste at source in FOUR separate streams: wet waste,
  dry waste, sanitary waste, and special care waste. Hand over segregated
  waste to authorised waste pickers/collectors as directed by local
  authorities.
- Wrap used sanitary waste (diapers, sanitary pads, etc.) securely in
  manufacturer-provided pouches or suitable wrapping before placing in the
  sanitary waste bin, separate from dry/wet/special care bins.
- Store construction and demolition waste separately on their own premises.
- Store horticulture/garden waste separately on their own premises.
- NOT throw, burn, or bury solid waste on streets or open public spaces, or
  dispose of it in drains or water bodies.
- Keep suitable containers for storage of segregated waste (wet, dry,
  special care, sanitary) for handover to authorised collectors.
- Not mix bio-medical waste with solid waste.

--- GENERAL PRINCIPLE ---

Waste hierarchy (priority order for managing solid waste): prevention is
most preferred, followed by reduction, reuse, recycling, recovery, and
disposal (landfill) is the least preferred option.
